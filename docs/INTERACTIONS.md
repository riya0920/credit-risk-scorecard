# ML-3 — interactions, and the proxy a cross can smuggle in

Generated by `run_interactions.py` on 26,314 real HMDA 2023 Delaware
applications.

A WoE scorecard is **additive by construction**. Each feature becomes
its weight of evidence and the pieces are summed in log-odds space, so
the card can say *high LTV is worse* and *high DTI is worse* and it
cannot say *high LTV is worse when DTI is also high*. An interaction is
precisely the statement the functional form cannot make.

## 1. Does the additive assumption actually cost anything here?

| LTV | DTI | applications | share | observed denial | additive predicts | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| >95 | >43 | 2,536 | 9.6% | 28.47% | 36.63% | **-8.16 pp** |
| <=80 | >43 | 5,725 | 21.8% | 40.72% | 37.65% | **+3.07 pp** |
| >95 | <30 | 372 | 1.4% | 33.06% | 18.47% | **+14.59 pp** |
| 80-90 | >43 | 1,538 | 5.8% | 46.03% | 42.85% | **+3.19 pp** |
| <=80 | <30 | 3,932 | 14.9% | 17.96% | 19.14% | **-1.18 pp** |
| 80-90 | 36-43 | 963 | 3.7% | 13.91% | 16.59% | **-2.68 pp** |
| >95 | 30-36 | 575 | 2.2% | 17.22% | 13.57% | **+3.65 pp** |
| <=80 | 36-43 | 3,593 | 13.7% | 14.22% | 13.81% | **+0.41 pp** |

The largest weighted gap is **-8.16 percentage points** on the
`>95 x >43` cell, which holds 9.6% of applications.

Ranked by surprise **weighted by population**. An unweighted ranking
puts the thinnest cells on top, because a cell of forty applicants can
be surprising by pure sampling — and the top of a report is where a
reader looks.

## 2. What the cross costs in cells

| cross | cells | above the 1% floor | population that would merge to OTHER |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ltv_band` x `dti_band` | 16 | 16 | 0.0% |
| `ltv_band` x `income_band` | 16 | 16 | 0.0% |
| `dti_band` x `income_band` | 16 | 16 | 0.0% |

**Every cell survives the floor here, so this cost does not bite
on these crosses** — 4x4 on 26,314 applications leaves every cell
populated. Reported anyway rather than dropped, because it is the
number to check BEFORE deciding a cross is worth it and the answer
happens to be favourable this time.

Where it does bite is a cross of two genuinely high-cardinality
features — a 12-level purpose against a 20-level property type is
240 cells, most below the floor. Those merge into OTHER, and the
interaction that motivated the cross disappears into a bucket, so
the cross buys nothing but degrees of freedom.

## 3. The check a per-feature fairness screen cannot perform

Both inputs are legitimate underwriting variables. Neither is a
protected attribute. The question is whether their **intersection**
identifies a group.

Measured against 22,425 applications with race reported.

**`ltv_band` x `dti_band`** — 0 cell(s) at or above 3x concentration


**`ltv_band` x `income_band`** — 0 cell(s) at or above 3x concentration


**`dti_band` x `income_band`** — 0 cell(s) at or above 3x concentration


### Reading this result

**No cell reaches 3x on this data.** That is the honest
outcome and it is worth stating plainly rather than hunting for a
cross that does fire. Delaware's HMDA population is not
segregated finely enough along LTV, DTI and income for a 4x4 cross
of them to isolate a racial group.

A null result from a control is still a result. What it does not
license is the conclusion that crossing features is safe in
general — it is a statement about these three variables on this
state's book, and `run_redlining.py` already showed that
**geography** on the same data does carry group information.
Crossing a geographic band into a card is the case this screen
exists for.

## 4. The cross that does carry group information

`run_redlining.py` found that tract minority share predicts denial even
after controlling for the applicant's own group. So geography is the
input where a cross is most likely to build a proxy — and it is
routinely present in a card as a region code or a property-type band.

`tract_band` x `ltv_band` on 21,931 applications: **0** cell(s) at
or above 3x.

None reaches the threshold either, on the tract bands available
here. Delaware has 19 majority-minority tracts in this extract,
which is a thin base for a cross — the screen is reporting the
limits of the sample as much as the state of the market.

## 5. A spurious finding this screen produced, and the fix

**The first version of this run reported two proxy cells. Both were
noise, and the screen was wrong rather than the data.**

| cell | applicants | group | in cell | group share of book | reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `90-95 x <60k` | 233 | Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander | **2** | 0.2% | 3.6x |
| `50%+ x >95` | 1,125 | 2 or more minority races | **18** | 0.5% | 3.2x |

The first cell's group is 0.2% of the book, so its expected count in a
233-applicant cell is **half a person**. Two applicants produced a
3.6x ratio. That is not a proxy; it is a rounding error with a ratio
attached.

The screen floored the CELL size and not the GROUP COUNT inside it —
and a ratio is unstable exactly where its denominator is small, which
a cell-size floor does not constrain. `MIN_GROUP_COUNT = 30` closes
it, and both findings disappear.

Worth recording because the failure is invisible in a synthetic test:
a constructed proxy world has one group at 10% and a clear signal. It
takes real data, with a long tail of small groups, to produce a
confident 3.6x from two people.

## 6. A limitation of this screen, found by a test

Concentration is measured against the **book's own** mix, which makes
it self-referential. When a cell is a large fraction of the population,
filling it with a group raises the book's rate too and the ratio falls
back toward 1.

On a 2x2 cross each cell is a quarter of the book: loading one of them
75% with a group that starts at 10% takes the population to ~27% and
the cell to ~77% — a concentration of **2.9**, under the 3.0 threshold,
on a cell that is three-quarters one group.

So the screen is most sensitive where cells are small, which is where
proxies usually live, and least sensitive on coarse crosses. Fixing it
properly needs an external reference population rather than the book
itself. A test pins the behaviour so it is a known limitation rather
than a surprise.

## 7. What this does not establish

- **A concentrated cell is not proof of discrimination.** It is proof
  that a feature the model can use carries information about group
  membership. Whether the model then uses it, and with what effect, is
  the AIR and the redlining screen's question.
- **One state, one year, one lender pool.** Every caveat from
  `docs/HMDA_FAIR_LENDING.md` applies unchanged.
- **The bands are chosen.** LTV at 80/90/95 follows mortgage-insurance
  conventions and DTI at 36/43 follows QM, so they are not arbitrary —
  but different cuts would give different cells, and a screen that is
  sensitive to banding is sensitive to a modelling choice.
