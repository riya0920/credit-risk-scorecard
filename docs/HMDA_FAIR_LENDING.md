# ML-3 — fair lending on REAL data (HMDA 2023, Delaware)

Generated by `run_hmda_fair_lending.py`. Unlike every other number in
this project, **the protected attributes here are real** — reported by
applicants under the Home Mortgage Disclosure Act and published by the
CFPB. Free, no account.

## 0. What this can and cannot answer

**HMDA records the lender's DECISION, not the score behind it.** So an
AIR computed here measures disparity in observed outcomes across every
lender in the state — a market statistic, not an audit of one model.
Presenting it as an audit would be the central error of the exercise.

**There is no credit score in HMDA.** Since 2018 it carries DTI, LTV
and property value, which is far more than the older data, and it still
lacks the single most predictive underwriting variable. Every disparity
below is therefore an **upper bound on unexplained disparity**: some of
it is credit risk this file cannot see.

The 80% rule compares each group against the **best-performing** group,
which is how the EEOC formulates it and is the only reference that
needs no defence — it is the treatment the market demonstrably can give.

## 3. The binary cut, with an interval

| | |
|---|---|
| approval rate, White | 77.48% |
| approval rate, all other reported groups | 65.69% |
| **AIR** | **0.8478** |
| bootstrap 95% CI | [0.8333, 0.8625] |
| 80% rule | passes |

The interval matters more here than it did on synthetic data: the 80%
rule gets applied to a point estimate as though it were exact, and a
0.79 on a thin sample is not the same finding as a 0.79 on a fat one.

### And here is why section 2 comes first

**This binary AIR passes at 0.8478. Four separate groups in section 2
fail.**

- Black or African American: 62.27% approval, AIR **0.7846**, n = 5,394
- American Indian or Alaska Native: 39.02% approval, AIR **0.4917**, n = 123
- 2 or more minority races: 56.67% approval, AIR **0.7139**, n = 120
- Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander: 50.00% approval, AIR **0.6299**, n = 62

Rolling every non-White applicant into one bucket averages a
39.02% approval rate together with a 79.37% one and reports the
mean. The disparity did not go anywhere — it was averaged away, and
the aggregate number would have closed the file.

This is the same masking effect ML-1 demonstrates on constructed
data with its intersectional cross. Here it is on 29,929 real
applications, and it is the single strongest argument in this
repository for reporting group-by-group before reporting a summary.

## 4. What survives controlling for the underwriting variables?

The disparity above is raw. The question a lender is actually asked is
whether it survives the legitimate factors — so this fits a denial
model on DTI, LTV, income, loan amount and loan-to-income, and then
asks whether group still predicts denial **on top of** that.

| model | features | AUC |
|---|---|---|
| underwriting only | 5 | 0.7612 |
| + group indicator | 6 | 0.7695 |

Group coefficient (log-odds of denial): **+0.5300** — an odds ratio of
**1.699**.

**Not zero.** After controlling for every underwriting variable HMDA
gives us, group still moves the odds of denial by 69.9%.

That is the finding, and here is what it is not: proof of
discrimination. The missing credit score sits exactly where this
residual lives, and any variable correlated with both group and
risk will show up here whether or not any lender did anything
wrong. It is grounds to investigate, which is precisely what a
regulator's screen is for — a screen is a question, not a verdict.

AUC barely moves (+0.0084), which is its own point: a variable can be
statistically significant and add almost nothing to prediction. Reading
the AUC delta as 'group does not matter' would be reading the wrong
number.

## 6. What is still not built

- **A credit score.** Not in HMDA, and it is the variable that would
  turn an upper bound into an estimate.
- **One lender.** This is state-wide across all filers, so it is a
  market statistic. Auditing a single institution means filtering to
  its LEI and accepting a much smaller sample.
- **Redlining analysis.** HMDA carries census tract, which is the
  input for geographic disparity work this does not do.
- **Pricing disparity.** `interest_rate` is present on 73% of rows and
  originations only; rate spread analysis is a separate exercise.
