# ML-3 — geographic disparity (redlining screen), HMDA 2023 Delaware

Generated by `run_redlining.py`. **A different question from the AIR:**
the AIR asks whether individual applicants of one group fare worse;
this asks whether *places* do. They come apart — a lender can treat
every applicant identically and still decline a neighbourhood by not
lending in it.

## 1. Tracts, and the ones this refuses to rank

| | |
|---|---|
| tracts in the file | 255 |
| tracts with >= 30 applications | 248 |
| tracts excluded as too thin | 7 (2.7% of tracts, 0.4% of applications) |

A tract with four applications and one denial has a 25% denial rate and
means nothing. Ranking on it puts noise at the top of the report, which
is exactly where the reader looks — so thin tracts are excluded and the
**exclusion is reported**, because dropping 0.4% of applications
silently would be choosing the sample after seeing it.

## 2. Denial rate by tract minority share

| tract minority share | tracts | applications | denial rate | median income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| <20% | 114 | 12,808 | 22.14% | $103k |
| 20-50% | 115 | 14,775 | 29.84% | $84k |
| 50-80% | 19 | 1,940 | 32.11% | $70k |

Spread between the highest and lowest band: **10.0 percentage
points**.

**And look at the income column before reading anything into that.**
Tract minority share correlates with tract income, so a raw
geographic gradient is partly an income gradient. A disparity that
survives no controls at all is not evidence of redlining — it is
evidence that poorer areas get declined more, which everyone
already knows and which is not by itself unlawful.

## 3. Does tract composition still predict denial after controls?

| model | AUC |
|---|---|
| underwriting only | 0.7549 |
| + applicant's own group | 0.7591 |
| + tract minority share | 0.7595 |

With both in the model:

| term | coefficient | odds ratio |
|---|---|---|
| applicant is minority | +0.3459 | 1.413 |
| tract minority share | +0.3306 | 1.392 |

**The applicant's own group is in the model first, on purpose.**
Without it the tract term absorbs individual-level disparity and gets
reported as geography — which is the ecological fallacy with a
regression in front of it.

The tract term survives. That is a **screen result**: after
controlling for the applicant's own underwriting profile AND their
own group, the composition of the neighbourhood still moves the
odds of denial by 39.2%.

It is not a finding of redlining, and the reasons are the same as
everywhere else in this project: no credit score, no property
condition, no loan officer, and this is every lender in the state
pooled rather than one institution's conduct.

## 4. What this still is not

- **Not one lender.** Redlining is an institution's conduct. This pools
  every filer in the state, so it can identify a market pattern and
  cannot attribute it.
- **No application-rate denominator.** The strongest redlining evidence
  is not the denial rate in a tract — it is the near-absence of
  applications from it, which means measuring marketing and branch
  presence against tract population. HMDA alone cannot see that.
- **No peer comparison.** The standard method compares one lender
  against similarly situated peers in the same geography, and that
  needs a peer-group definition this does not have.
